Key rules for a Korean CV

  • Photo: Traditionally included on Korean resumes, though blind-hiring practices have reduced this at many employers, especially in the public sector.
  • Length: One to two pages; many large companies use their own application forms.
  • Personal details: Date of birth, contact details and education are traditional, but many employers now accept less personal information. Follow the application form.

Sections to include

Personal information, education, work experience, certifications, language scores (for example TOEIC), and a self-introduction.

Extra tips

  • Large Korean companies often require their own online form and a self-introduction essay.
  • Language test scores such as TOEIC or OPIc are commonly listed.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the self-introduction when it is requested.
  • Ignoring the company's own application form.
