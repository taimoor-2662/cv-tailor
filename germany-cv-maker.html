Germany / Austria / Switzerland CV Format and Free CV Maker

The German-style CV (Lebenslauf) is a tabular, reverse-chronological document. Choose German as the CV language for German employers.

Key rules for a German-speaking countries CV

  • Photo: Common but not required. A professional headshot is traditional, though many employers now accept CVs without one.
  • Length: One to two pages in a tabular, reverse-chronological layout.
  • Personal details: Traditional CVs list date of birth and nationality, and in Switzerland often the residence permit. These are optional and increasingly left out; include them if the employer expects them.

Sections to include

Personal data, work experience, education, skills and languages, and a place and date (and signature) at the end, which is often optional today.

Extra tips

  • Send supporting documents such as references and certificates when you apply; employers often expect them.
  • Explain any gaps in the timeline briefly.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving unexplained gaps in the timeline.
  • Sending a creative layout to a conservative employer.
